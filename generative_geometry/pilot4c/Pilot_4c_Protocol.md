# Generative Geometry — Pilot 4c Protocol

## Stability-Guided Constructor Refinement and Developmental Scaffolding

### Status

Protocol fixed on 2026-11-23 before implementation, smoke testing, or the full experiment. Numerical results must not be used to revise the hypotheses, search rules, controls, primary endpoint, or interpretation criteria in this document.

## Research question

Can a system improve an imperfect relational constructor by repeatedly revising its expression, while retaining the parent unless a variant passes validity, stability, and robust-development criteria, and can the resulting constructor improve performance on a held-out task?

The experiment separately asks:

1. **Refinement:** does the refined expression outperform the fixed initial expression?
2. **Guidance:** does robust development guidance outperform a random lineage?
3. **Scaffolding:** does building on accepted intermediate expressions outperform a matched search that cannot use those intermediates as parents?
4. **Lineage value:** does refinement outperform independent label-guided search with the same candidate-evaluation budget?

Pilot 4c tests actual syntactic revision of a constructor. It does not infer refinement from coefficient learning, regression improvement, or sequential addition of unchanged constructors.

## Scope and interpretation

The experiment concerns search and revision within a supplied expression grammar. It does not test invention of the underlying geometric language, unrestricted open-endedness, information creation, or consciousness-like reflection. Developmental scaffolding means only that an accepted intermediate expression causally serves as the parent of a later expression and that removing this parent-chaining mechanism impairs the matched control.

## Geometry and data separation

The six-point configurations, normalization, random rotations, targets, and four environment families match Pilot 4b:

- `convex_radial`;
- `interior_points`;
- `irregular_radial`;
- `elongated`.

Each family has 1,200 training-pool configurations and 800 test configurations per replicate. Final evaluation uses leave-one-family-out transfer. Constructor search for a final fold can access only the other three families; the final test family is excluded from search, validity checks, model selection, and refinement.

### Development tasks

Only these targets guide refinement:

- hull perimeter;
- compactness;
- hull vertex count.

Compactness is geometrically related to area, so development guidance is not independent of geometric extent. Nevertheless, hull-area labels themselves are excluded from search and selection.

### Held-out final task

- convex-hull area.

After a constructor has been fixed, hull-area labels from the three training families may be used to fit the final regressor.

## Expression grammar

For each unordered point triple `(i,j,k)`, anchored at `i`, define:

\[
u=p_j-p_i,\qquad v=p_k-p_i,\qquad w=p_k-p_j.
\]

The supplied local leaves are:

- `dot` — \(u\cdot v\);
- `cross` — \(u_xv_y-u_yv_x\);
- `u2` — \(\|u\|^2\);
- `v2` — \(\|v\|^2\);
- `w2` — \(\|w\|^2\).

Unary operations are:

- `abs`;
- `square`;
- `sqrt_abs`.

Binary operations are:

- `multiply`;
- `divide`;
- `add`;
- `abs_diff`.

Division is defined throughout as:

\[
\operatorname{divide}(a,b)=\frac{a}{|b|+10^{-8}}.
\]

Expressions are trees with maximum depth 2, where a leaf has depth 0. Each expression is applied uniformly to all 20 unordered point triples, producing a 20-component constructor block. The supplied-area reference remains `abs(cross)`, up to an irrelevant factor of \(1/2\), but it is not supplied as the initial or refined expression.

## Fixed initial constructor

Every search begins with:

```text
dot
```

This is a researcher-supplied, shallow, imperfect constructor. Fixing it across all runs isolates expression revision from initial-constructor discovery. It also makes at least two revisions necessary for the local path `dot` → `cross` → `abs(cross)`.

The initial constructor must pass the same label-free validity and stability gates as every variant. Failure of the initial gate is an implementation failure, not a negative experimental result.

## Mutation neighborhood

Given a current expression, its deterministic mutation neighborhood consists of unique expressions generated by the following operations:

1. Replace any leaf by a different leaf.
2. Change the operation at any existing unary or binary node.
3. Apply `abs`, `square`, or `sqrt_abs` to the root, if the depth limit permits.
4. Combine the root with a leaf using any binary operation, with the existing expression on either side, if the depth limit permits.
5. Remove a root unary operation.
6. Replace a root binary expression by either child.
7. Swap the children of a root binary expression.

No-op mutations and duplicate expressions are removed. The neighborhood is sorted lexicographically before evaluation so that ties are deterministic.

Mutations can therefore revise internal leaves and operations, not merely append fixed features. Every proposal records its parent expression.

## Label-free validity and stability gates

A proposal is gated before any development labels are consulted. The gate reference data comprise the development configurations from the three final-training families.

A constructor is rejected if any of the following holds:

1. **Numerical validity:** any output is non-finite or has absolute value greater than \(10^6\).
2. **Family near-constancy:** in any reference family, more than 20% of the 20 triple columns have variance
   \[
   \leq 10^{-10}(\mathrm{mean}(x)^2+10^{-12}).
   \]
3. **Small-sample near-constancy:** in any development fitting subset used at budget 40 or 80, more than 20% of triple columns meet the same near-constancy criterion.
4. **Family scale collapse:** the ratio between the largest and smallest family root-mean-square outputs exceeds 100, or the smallest family RMS is zero within numerical tolerance.

These checks use no target labels. In particular, they are intended to exclude trivial self-ratios and features that become nearly constant in small training samples. Gate rejection counts as one candidate evaluation, but the rejected expression receives no development score and cannot be retained.

## Robust development score

For each final leave-one-family-out fold, three internal development folds are constructed by leaving out one of the three available training families in turn.

For each internal fold:

- the other two families provide development fitting data;
- the internal held-out family provides 80 validation configurations;
- fitting uses nested total budgets 40 and 80, split equally between the two fitting families;
- the representation is 15 pairwise distances plus the candidate's 20-component block;
- the fixed ridge learner and training-only feature/target standardization match final evaluation;
- hull perimeter, compactness, and hull count are fit jointly as three development outputs.

Each task/fold/budget validation \(R^2\) is clipped to \([-1,1]\) for development scoring only. The constructor score is the median of all 18 values: three tasks × three internal folds × two budgets. Clipping and the median are pre-specified protections against catastrophic extrapolation dominating search. Raw unclipped development scores are also logged.

A larger robust development score is better. No hull-area value enters this score.

## Refined-lineage procedure

1. Score the initial `dot` constructor.
2. Generate its complete mutation neighborhood.
3. Evaluate proposals in deterministic order, applying the gates before development scoring.
4. Select the valid proposal with the highest robust development score. Ties are resolved lexicographically.
5. Accept it only if its score exceeds the current parent's score by more than \(10^{-4}\).
6. If accepted, the child replaces the parent as the current expression. The parent remains stored in the lineage archive and can be inspected, but is not added as another feature block.
7. If no proposal passes the improvement threshold, stop and retain the current parent.
8. Permit at most four accepted-revision rounds.

The final representation therefore remains 35-dimensional throughout refinement: 15 pairwise features plus one current 20-component constructor block. Candidate-evaluation counts include every gated proposal, including rejected and duplicate proposals; no cached score is treated as a free evaluation.

## Matched controls

### 1. Initial

The fixed `dot` expression receives no revision. This is the primary baseline for detecting actual improvement from expression refinement.

### 2. Independent search

This control has development-label guidance but no lineage constraint.

- It receives exactly the same number of candidate evaluations used by refined search in that replicate/final-fold run.
- Each proposal is sampled independently from a fixed stochastic expression generator over the same leaves, operations, and depth-2 grammar.
- The generator chooses a leaf, unary node, or binary node with probabilities 0.40, 0.25, and 0.35 when depth permits; at depth 2 it must emit a leaf.
- The best valid expression by robust development score is retained, with `dot` as the incumbent.
- It does not use the refined lineage's current expression or accepted intermediates.

This control tests whether parent-chained refinement has an advantage over equally budgeted, label-guided non-lineage search. It is not described as uniform sampling over all possible expressions.

### 3. Unscaffolded search

This control has the same robust development guidance and candidate budget but cannot build on intermediate revisions.

- Every proposal is a one-mutation variant of the original `dot` root.
- The best-scoring variant may be retained as output, but it never becomes a parent.
- If the refined budget exceeds the size of the root neighborhood, proposals are cycled and reevaluated so that the evaluation budget remains matched.

The control isolates the causal contribution of using accepted intermediates as parents. It does not represent all possible nonrecursive search algorithms.

### 4. Random lineage

This control preserves lineage-like expression change but removes development-label guidance.

- It starts from `dot` and uses the same mutation grammar, depth limit, gates, number of rounds, and per-round proposal counts as the refined run.
- At each proposal slot, a neighbor of the current random-lineage expression is sampled uniformly and checked by the label-free gates.
- The last valid sampled proposal in a round becomes the next current expression, regardless of development performance.
- If no valid proposal occurs, the current expression is retained.

The random lineage's final expression is used; no development-score selection is applied afterward.

## Final representations

Eight representations are evaluated:

- `pairwise` — 15 pairwise distances;
- `initial` — pairwise plus `dot`;
- `refined` — pairwise plus the final refined expression;
- `independent_search` — pairwise plus the matched independent-search result;
- `unscaffolded` — pairwise plus the matched unscaffolded result;
- `random_lineage` — pairwise plus the random-lineage endpoint;
- `supplied_area` — pairwise plus `abs(cross)`;
- `raw_quadratic` — raw coordinates and all quadratic monomials.

Except for `pairwise`, `supplied_area`, and `raw_quadratic`, constructor-based representations have 35 dimensions. The final area task and test family play no role in choosing among them.

## Final evaluation

- 20 replicates and four leave-one-family-out folds;
- nested training budgets 25, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1000;
- fixed `Ridge(alpha=1e-3)`;
- training-only feature and target standardization;
- common training order across representations within each replicate/fold;
- held-out-family MSE, NRMSE, \(R^2\), and \(\log(1+\mathrm{NRMSE})\).

The primary endpoint is budget 200. Other budgets are secondary sample-efficiency analyses.

## Lineage and constructor-content logging

For every proposal, the experiment records:

- replicate, final test family, and search condition;
- round and candidate-evaluation number;
- parent and proposed expression;
- validity status and gate-reason codes;
- robust and unclipped development scores when evaluated;
- acceptance or retention decision.

For every accepted refinement, the archive records parent, child, score improvement, expression depth, and whether the parent is structurally embedded in the child. The analysis reports exact area rediscovery, full revision paths, number of accepted revisions, terminal expressions, gate-rejection frequencies, and performance stratified by direct versus indirect area discovery.

Syntactic novelty alone is not treated as mathematical novelty. Expressions related by scaling, sign, or simple transformations of the cross product remain area-related.

## Pre-specified statistical analysis

Because Pilot 4b revealed heavy-tailed failures, the primary paired analysis uses:

\[
L=\log(1+\mathrm{NRMSE}),
\]

with lower values indicating better performance. For each replicate, \(L\) is averaged across the four final test families. A paired comparison averages the within-replicate differences and reports a two-sided 95% Student-\(t\) interval over the 20 replicate values.

For comparisons against refined search, the difference is defined as:

\[
L_{\mathrm{comparator}}-L_{\mathrm{refined}},
\]

so a positive value favors refinement.

### Primary comparisons at budget 200

1. refined versus initial — evidence of useful expression refinement;
2. refined versus random lineage — evidence of development-guidance value;
3. refined versus unscaffolded — evidence for causal scaffolding by intermediate parents.

### Secondary comparisons

- refined versus independent search — lineage advantage beyond equally budgeted guided search;
- refined versus supplied area — recovery of the known constructor's effectiveness;
- refined versus pairwise and raw quadratic — contextual representation comparisons.

Mean and median \(R^2\), median NRMSE, per-family results, replicate win fractions, and exact two-sided sign tests are supporting analyses. They do not replace a failed primary interval criterion. Secondary comparisons are not adjusted for multiplicity and will be identified accordingly.

## Interpretation rules fixed before testing

### Positive refinement result

Useful refinement requires:

- at least one accepted syntactic revision in the lineages; and
- a positive refined-versus-primary-initial interval for paired log error at budget 200.

Improved development score alone is insufficient.

### Positive guidance result

Development guidance is supported only if the refined-versus-random-lineage paired log-error interval is positive.

### Positive scaffolding result

Causal scaffolding is supported only if:

- accepted intermediates occur on recorded ancestry paths; and
- the refined-versus-unscaffolded paired log-error interval is positive.

This supports the contribution of parent chaining under this mutation grammar, not a general claim that all development requires scaffolding.

### No lineage-search advantage

If refined search does not outperform independent search, the experiment must not claim that lineage-based refinement is superior to equally budgeted independent search. Refinement may still have occurred relative to the initial constructor.

### Incomplete recovery

If supplied area outperforms refined constructors, refinement has not fully recovered the supplied representation's effectiveness, even when refinement improves on its starting point.

### Negative and mixed results

No improvement over initial, no advantage over random lineage, or no advantage over unscaffolded search will be reported as failure of the corresponding claim. Instability, catastrophic tails, early stopping, failure to discover area, and condition-specific reversals will be retained rather than removed from the analysis.

## Limitations

- The initial constructor, grammar, operations, development tasks, and learner are researcher-supplied.
- The search space is finite in practice because of the depth limit, although many syntactic expressions are possible.
- Compactness makes development guidance geometrically related to area.
- The experiment tests one starting constructor and one geometric setting.
- Candidate evaluation is matched by count, not wall-clock time or memory.
- The unscaffolded control tests parent chaining within the specified local mutation process, not every imaginable nonrefinement algorithm.
- Expression equivalence and conceptual novelty are not established by syntax alone.
- Final statistical intervals treat replicates, not individual folds, as the independent units.
- Positive results would demonstrate a computational mechanism of stability-guided revision and scaffolding, not a metaphysical conclusion.
